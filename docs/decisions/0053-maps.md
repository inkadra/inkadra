# 0053 · Map cards and GPX routes

Proposed, 2026-11. Extends the board document (decision 0052), files and media (decisions 0009
and 0048), guest fields (decision 0013) and the content security policy. Issue #319.

**Context.** Location scouting and event coverage use maps all the time: a pinned place, a race
route, a meeting point. Location cards (decision 0044) only keep a link, on purpose: no tiles, no
geocoding, nothing new for the CSP. A map preview needs map data from somewhere, and the obvious
way, a map library in the browser loading tiles, would make every person who opens a board, and
every guest of a share link, contact a third party, with their address and what they look at.

**Decision.**
- **Static pictures, made once by the server.** When someone pastes a map link, types an address
  or uploads a GPX file, the server fetches the tiles around the place from the tile server of the
  instance (about twenty 256 px tiles), composites them with sharp into one 1024 × 560 picture and
  stores it as an image asset of the project. Browsers, guests and PDF and image exports only ever
  load that picture, from Inkadra. No map library in the web app, no tile host in the CSP, and the
  tile server sees only the server, once per map, after a person's action.
- **Configurable and optional.** `MAP_TILE_URL` (default the OpenStreetMap tiles, no API key) and
  `MAP_TILE_ATTRIBUTION` (shown on every map picture, from `Instance.maps`). `MAPS=false` turns
  pictures off: map cards keep the name, address and "Open in Maps" link, GPX files their route
  line on a plain surface. The server sends `Inkadra/<version> (+PUBLIC_URL)` as user agent, as
  the OpenStreetMap tile policy asks. Each process asks for at most six tiles a second, queued,
  and keeps the last 400 tiles (32 MB at most) in memory, so a retried job or a second map of
  the same place fetches nothing again; a map whose tiles do not all arrive is not stored. Tile
  and geocoder requests follow no redirect and are refused past their size cap (1 MB a tile, 256
  KB an answer), by the declared length before reading.
- **Geocoding is off by default.** `MAP_GEOCODER_URL` takes a Nominatim-compatible `/search`
  endpoint. Unset, a typed address makes a card with the address and a search link and no
  picture; set, the address is sent there once, when the card is made.
- **Map links are read, not fetched.** `parseMapLink` (in `@inkadra/shared`) reads coordinates,
  zoom and name from Google Maps, Apple Maps and OpenStreetMap links. Only short links
  (`maps.app.goo.gl`) are followed, with the SSRF-safe fetch of link previews.
- **Document version 11** adds the `map` element: `name`, optional `address`, `url` (the link,
  opened by "Open in Maps"; otherwise OpenStreetMap at the place or a search for the address),
  `lat`, `lon`, `zoom` and `imageAssetId`. Guests see all of it: a map card is something the team
  chose to show, unlike the address of a location card.
- **GPX files** are untrusted XML. The worker reads them with htmlparser2 in XML mode, which never
  loads a DTD or an external entity, up to 20 MB, 200,000 points and 10,000 segments. It stores
  `asset.track`
  (database migration 0021, `Asset.track` in the API): the length (great-circle sum), the climb
  (rises of at least 3 m from the last low point, so GPS jitter adds nothing; null without
  elevations) and the line, at most 512 points in fractions of the picture. With maps on, the
  map around the route becomes the file's thumbnails. The card draws the line, a green dot at
  the start and a red one at the end, with the tokens of the theme, and says
  "GPX · 25 km · +1050 m".
- **Guests** receive `track` (length, climb and the drawn line) with the GPX files of the cards
  they see, even when the share link does not allow downloads: the card shows the route anyway,
  as audio of an animatic streams without a download. The original file stays behind the
  download permission.
- **Location cards** keep their link only for now; showing the same map there is a follow-up.

**Consequences.** Every map is a small image in the project's storage and counts against it.
Maps stay as they were when made: a tile server change does not redraw them. Instances that make
many maps should point `MAP_TILE_URL` at their own or a commercial tile server.

## Exploring a map (#373)

Added 2026-11.

**Context.** A picture is enough to recognise a place, not to look around it: where the car
park is, how the route gets to the pass. People asked to pan and zoom the map of a card without
leaving the board, and without giving up what the pictures bought: browsers and guests talk to
Inkadra only.

**Decision.**
- **On request, one at a time.** A map card, or a GPX file with its route, stays its picture.
  "Explore" over the picture (or a double click on a map card) turns it into a map that pans and
  zooms inside the same area; Esc, a press outside the card or exploring another map turns it
  back. While explored, the card drags from the 6 px frame around the map and from its title,
  as the inline player of link cards does (decision 0050), and it takes the
  same single turn: exploring a map stops a playing video or sound, and playing one ends it.
- **Leaflet, loaded on the first activation** with a dynamic import (about 40 KB gzipped, BSD-2-
  Clause); boards that nobody explores never download it. Its controls take the tokens of the
  theme, its animations stop with reduced motion, and the credit of the instance stays on the
  map. A place gets its pin; a route its line and the green and red dots, put back on the map
  from the line of the picture: `Asset.track.view` (new, optional) records the zoom and corner
  of that picture in Web Mercator pixels. Routes read before have no view and no "Explore".
- **Tiles through Inkadra.** `GET /api/v1/map-tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}` for people signed in, and
  `GET /api/v1/guest/links/{linkId}/map-tiles/{z}/{x}/{y}` for any guest of a working link
  (`GuestSession.maps` tells the guest page that maps are on, and their credit). Tiles are public
  map data, so neither route checks which cards someone can see. The server checks the path
  (whole numbers, zoom 1–19, `x` and `y` inside the world), fetches through the same queue
  (six a second per process), cache, user agent, `redirect: 'error'` and size cap as the
  pictures, serves only bytes that are a PNG, JPEG or WebP, and lets browsers keep them a day
  (`private`). Each person or guest gets 600 tiles a minute; a tile that would wait more than
  five seconds in the queue is refused with 429, so a busy instance shows blank squares rather
  than a growing queue. `MAPS=false` answers 404 and hides "Explore".
- **Phones** follow Leaflet: one finger pans an explored map, two zoom it; the board around it
  still pans and pinches as before, since nothing is interactive until "Explore" is pressed.

**Consequences.** Exploring sends many more requests to the tile server than pictures did: a
few dozen per view, cached for everyone in the process. The OpenStreetMap tile policy allows
interactive use with a cache and a user agent, but instances whose people explore a lot should
point `MAP_TILE_URL` at their own or a commercial tile server.
